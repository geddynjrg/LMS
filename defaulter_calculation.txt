### Why Only 19 Months Defaulted (Not 126 Months Since 2015)?

The service **intentionally caps** the calculation at the original repayment period (24 months):

```php
$monthsInRepayment = min($repaymentStart->diffInMonths($today), 24);
```

This is correct for most loan systems because:
- After the contractual period ends (2017-07-09), the loan is **overdue as a whole**.
- But the **defaulted months** are only counted for the **scheduled repayment period** (24 months).
- The remaining arrears (80,000) represent **19 missed monthly payments** during those 24 months.
- The extra time since 2017 (8+ years) is **accumulated overdue**, not additional "defaulted months" — it's just **worsening arrears**, not new missed installments.

In simple terms:
- The borrower was supposed to pay 24 installments.
- They only paid ~4.8 installments (20,000 / 4,166.67).
- They missed **19 installments** → `months_defaulted = 19`

### Is This Behavior Desired?

**Yes — in most microfinance/loan tracking systems**, defaulted months are counted against the **original repayment schedule**, not forever.  
After the due date passes, the loan is simply "overdue" or "in default" (flag = true), but the "months defaulted" metric stops growing at the contractual end.

This prevents absurd numbers like "126 months defaulted" on very old loans.

### If You Want to Change It

If you prefer **unlimited defaulted months** (count every month since repayment start until fully paid):

Remove the `min(..., 24)` cap:

```php
$monthsInRepayment = $repaymentStart->diffInMonths($today);
```

Then `months_defaulted` would be **much higher** for old loans (e.g. ~126 in your example).

But I recommend **keeping the cap** — it's standard and prevents misleading metrics.
